Appendix B. More Technology Solutions Beyond Xona

Each entry is one way to keep working when GPS fails, in plain terms, with the caveat.

Low-orbit satellites

Iridium PNT chip

Delivers secure time and location from Iridium's existing satellites. On sale since July 2026, and Iridium says 150+ organizations have asked about it (Iridium, 2026). Iridium claims its signal is up to 1,000 times stronger than GPS (Iridium) [36].

CaveatThese are company claims.
Ground-based radio

NextNav

A ground network in the 902–928 MHz band for positioning and timing (FCC, 2024) [24].

CaveatContested at the FCC. Railroads, utilities, and toll operators object (Broadband Breakfast, 2026) [25], and critics say it needs only about 5% of the spectrum it seeks (R Street, 2026) [29].
Signed satellite signals

Galileo OSNMA (Europe)

Each message carries a cryptographic signature so receivers can reject fakes. Live since July 24, 2025 (European Commission, 2025) [37].

CaveatIt is Europe's system, not GPS.
Magnetic navigation

SandboxAQ AQNav

Reads Earth's magnetic field like a map, with AI filtering out the aircraft's own magnetic noise. Stayed within a standard accuracy tolerance for 100% of 150+ flight hours (The Quantum Insider, 2025) [38].

CaveatThat result is company-reported, and a later drone test disclosed no accuracy data (Runtime Wire, 2026) [39].
Camera-based navigation

Ukraine's drones

Onboard AI matches camera images against maps when jamming cuts off GPS (The Defense Post, 2026) [40].

CaveatBattlefield use, with few public details.
Detection software

Machine-learning detectors

About 96% detection on drones in the study in the body (Gormus et al., 2026) [18]. In a driving simulation, a small deliberate shift in a spoofed position cut one detector's accuracy from 99.9% to 20.4% (PMC study) [41], and spoofing has also worked against AI-based drone navigation (arXiv, 2025) [42].

CaveatSimulations and controlled tests only. I found no field evidence of AI-enhanced spoofing.
What DOT tested in 2020

11 technologies

Two low-orbit satellite systems, two fiber-optic timing systems, one map-matching and inertial system, and six ground radio systems (U.S. DOT, 2021, p. 7) [9].

CaveatA demonstration only, and DOT found no single system meets every need.

The key numbers come first, then the cases behind the headlines.

Rise between 2021 and 2024, per IATA (IATA, 2025) [6].

Gulf ships jammed per day

Persian Gulf shipping, June 15–18, 2025 (Windward, 2025) [33].

$1.4T · ~$1B/day
Economic stakes

Benefits since the 1980s, and the cost of a nationwide outage. The daily figure is a worst-case model; GPS outages have historically lasted under a day (RTI International, 2019) [3].

Cases

  • Flight 8243 (Dec. 2024). Jamming, then spoofing near Grozny. A missile struck the plane and killed 38. The interference was not the cause of the crash (FAA, 2026, p. 14) [4].
  • A pilot report, Amman. A crew judged a terrain alert false and did not start an escape maneuver, because their landing system and air traffic control agreed on their position (FAA, 2026) [4].
  • U.S. events. Interference near Denver and Dallas in 2022 (FAA, 2026, p. 10) [4], and a New Mexico air ambulance crash in May 2026 while military jamming was underway. The NTSB has not determined a cause (AVweb, 2026) [11].
  • Front Eagle (June 2025). The tanker collided with another tanker on June 17, during heavy Gulf jamming. Its operator blamed an “unspecified navigational error” (Windward, 2025) [33].
  • Baltic and Black Sea. Baltic interference has been almost daily since February 2022, and the Black Sea is described as a “gray zone” (Defense News, 2025 [34]; Spire, 2026 [35]).
Use with care: the Gulf jamming and the Front Eagle cause are not formally attributed. Counts from companies use different methods, so treat them as trends, not exact totals.

Appendix D. Likely Press Questions

Short answers the office can use if the press asks.

Is it safe to fly near conflict zones?

Pilots are trained to fall back on other navigation, and the FAA's guide walks them through it, but interference is rising and spoofing is hard to spot (FAA, 2026) [4].

Why don't we have a backup?

Congress ordered one in 2018 but made it subject to appropriations. DOT ran a $10 million demonstration and is still testing (U.S. DOT, 2021) [9].

Is the FCC about to give one company spectrum?

A draft rule is at the White House budget office, and a House committee moved to block it. Neither is final (Broadband Breakfast, 2026) [25].

Did jamming cause the Azerbaijan Airlines crash?

No. A missile struck the plane, though the crew was flying without GPS (FAA, 2026, p. 14) [4].

Doesn't our ally do the same thing as Russia?

Israel is widely reported to spoof defensively (ABC News, 2024) [31] and has pledged limits (Inside GNSS, 2026) [32]. We should push all states, including partners, toward the same limits.
